# Brand story

## Libre means freedom

For LibreSign, **Libre** means freedom to operate signing infrastructure, keep control of documents and data, inspect and adapt the software, integrate with existing systems, and choose how the solution is operated and maintained.

LibreSign's own source code is distributed as free software under **AGPL-3.0-or-later**. The brand therefore uses *Libre* in the sense of user freedom, not zero price.

That value can be expressed through concrete product benefits:

- self-hosting and infrastructure choice;
- data control and sovereignty;
- inspectable source code;
- interoperability and integration;
- adaptability to organizational workflows;
- reduced dependency on a single proprietary signing service;
- continuity when providers or operational models change.

## Brand promise

Electronic signing infrastructure should strengthen autonomy rather than create unnecessary dependency.

The brand should consistently connect four ideas:

- **freedom** — meaningful control over software, infrastructure, and data;
- **trust through verifiability** — evidence, inspectability, and precise claims rather than blind trust;
- **interoperability** — standards, integration, portability, and choice over lock-in;
- **continuity** — long-term operation and maintainability beyond a single provider.

## Free software culture

Free software is not merely a licensing attribute to mention in a footer. It is part of the project's identity.

Communication should make the practical freedoms understandable: people can run, study, adapt, share, and improve the software subject to its license. Professional services, paid support, hosting, integration, and sustainable economic activity are compatible with these freedoms.

The brand must not equate “Libre” with “free of charge”.

## Cooperative stewardship

LibreSign is developed and maintained by **LibreCode Cooperativa** together with contributors.

The cooperative context should be reflected in the project culture through shared responsibility, education and knowledge sharing, cooperation, long-term stewardship, and concern for the wider community.

LibreSign participation is not limited to cooperative membership. External contributors can participate through the project's public contribution processes; participation in the cooperative's economic and governance structure follows the cooperative's own rules.

## Cultural test

A LibreSign communication should feel:

- open rather than gatekept;
- precise rather than inflated;
- collaborative rather than proprietary;
- practical rather than ideological for its own sake;
- independent rather than tied to one mandatory provider;
- confident without making unverifiable claims.
