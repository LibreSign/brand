# Voice and tone

LibreSign should sound trustworthy, technically precise, privacy-aware, practical, and open to participation.

## Prefer
- explain control, interoperability, self-hosting, and auditability concretely;
- distinguish electronic-signature capabilities from legal guarantees;
- explain security and privacy with evidence and scope;
- connect software freedom to concrete user freedoms and operational choice;
- explain cooperative stewardship without implying that every contributor is a cooperative member;
- acknowledge contributors and community work accurately;
- speak to technical, business, legal, and public-sector audiences without overclaiming;
- make participation feel possible by documenting how the project works rather than presenting it as a closed vendor product.

## Avoid
- absolute claims such as “100% secure” or “legally valid everywhere”;
- implying that self-hosting alone guarantees compliance;
- hostile comparisons with proprietary competitors;
- vague sovereignty claims without explaining the operational mechanism;
- using “free” as a synonym for zero cost when describing software freedom;
- framing commercial activity and software freedom as inherently incompatible;
- implying endorsement, official status, partnership, or cooperative membership that does not exist.

## Brand behavior

The tone should make people want to participate because the project is understandable, useful, credible, and open — not because communication manufactures urgency, exclusivity, or superiority.

When choosing between spectacle and clarity, choose clarity.

When choosing between a proprietary-sounding promise and an inspectable fact, choose the fact.

The commercial narrative should connect software freedom to document control, infrastructure choice, integration, continuity, trust, and sustainable professional support.
